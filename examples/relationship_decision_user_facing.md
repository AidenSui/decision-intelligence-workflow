# Acceptance Example: Relationship Decision

This example demonstrates the default user-facing final answer format. It intentionally avoids exposing internal workflow labels.

## Sample User Decision

> I am deciding whether to continue this relationship. We have been together for three years. I care about my partner, but lately I feel lonely, criticized, and emotionally tired. Every serious conversation becomes defensive. I am afraid of wasting more time, but I am also afraid that leaving would be impulsive because I am anxious and burned out. My partner says they want to improve, but I am not sure anything will actually change. Should I stay or leave?

## User Summary

- Recommendation type: `test`
- Recommendation: Do not decide permanently today; run a bounded relationship repair test with explicit behavior changes and a stop-loss date.
- Core reason: The relationship still has stated willingness to improve, but the current pattern is not healthy enough to continue indefinitely without evidence of change.
- Biggest risk or objection: A repair test can become another way to delay leaving if the standards are vague.
- First action: Within 48 hours, define the three behaviors that must change and schedule one calm conversation about a 30-day repair window.

## The Real Decision

The surface question is "stay or leave." The more useful question is whether this relationship can produce observable emotional safety and repair, not just promises or intense conversations.

Feeling tired and lonely in the relationship is real information. It does not automatically mean leaving is the correct move today, but it does mean the current pattern should not be normalized.

## Options I See

- Stay without changing the structure: emotionally familiar, but likely to repeat the same pain.
- Leave immediately: clean and possibly right, but may be premature if both people have not tested concrete repair.
- Run a bounded repair test: gives the relationship a fair chance without letting ambiguity continue forever.
- Redesign the relationship expectations: clarify needs, conflict rules, emotional labor, and what each person is actually willing to change.

## Key Judgment

I would not optimize this as a simple "stay or leave" question yet, because that framing may hide the most important variable: whether your partner can participate in repair when the standards are specific.

The best next move is a bounded test with clear criteria. If the pattern changes, you gain evidence. If it does not, you gain permission to stop negotiating with hope.

## Strongest Objection

The strongest objection is that a "repair test" can become emotional limbo. If the relationship has already had many versions of "we will do better," another trial period may simply extend the same cycle.

That objection is valid. The test only makes sense if it has concrete behaviors, a short timeframe, and a pre-agreed stop condition. Without those, the recommendation should shift from `test` toward `reject`.

## Final Recommendation

Recommendation type: `test`

Run a 30-day relationship repair test before making a final stay-or-leave decision.

The bet is: if this relationship is repairable, there should be visible movement within 30 days in how conflict starts, how repair happens, and whether you feel emotionally safer. If there is no visible change, the issue is not just communication style; it is a repeated relational pattern.

## Test Plan

### 48 Hours

- Write down the three behaviors that must change for the relationship to feel viable.
- Decide your non-negotiable stop condition before talking, so the conversation does not become improvisation.
- Ask for one calm conversation, not a verdict conversation.

### 7 Days

- Have the conversation and propose a 30-day repair window.
- Agree on 2-3 observable behaviors, such as no insults, no stonewalling, repair within 24 hours, or one weekly check-in.
- Decide whether outside help is needed, such as couples counseling or a structured conversation guide.

### 30 Days

- Review evidence, not intentions: what actually changed, what repeated, and how your body feels around the relationship.
- Decide whether to continue, extend with stronger support, or leave.
- Do not extend the test unless there is real improvement and both people can name what changed.

### Later / Optional

- Consider couples counseling if both people are already showing effort.
- Create a shared conflict rule document if the repair window is working.
- Take individual space if conversations keep escalating.

## Communication Script

"I do not want this to be a fight or a breakup threat. I care about us, but I also need to be honest that the current pattern is hurting me. I am not asking for a perfect relationship in 30 days; I am asking whether we can show real repair when we are specific about what needs to change. The main things I need are less criticism, less defensiveness, and a clearer way to reconnect after conflict. I want us to define together what improvement would look like and what would mean this is not working. If we cannot agree on those standards, that itself is important information. I am willing to try, but I do not want us to keep surviving on vague hope."

## Stop Conditions

- Stop the test if criticism, contempt, threats, or emotional intimidation escalate.
- Do not continue if your partner agrees in words but refuses any concrete behavioral standard.
- Do not extend the test if 30 days produce apologies but no pattern change.
- Leave sooner if the relationship becomes unsafe or your mental health sharply worsens.

## What Would Make This Wrong

This advice would change if there is abuse, coercive control, serious safety risk, repeated betrayal, or a long history of failed repair attempts with no accountability. In those cases, a repair test may be too generous, and the recommendation may shift toward leaving or getting outside support first.
