# Acceptance Example: Resigning To Do AI Consulting

This example demonstrates the default user-facing final answer format. It intentionally avoids exposing internal workflow labels.

## Sample User Decision

> I am considering quitting my current software engineering job to start doing AI consulting. I feel burned out and under-recognized at work. I believe AI consulting has a big market, and I want more freedom. My wife is worried about income stability. We have some savings, but not enough for a long runway. Should I quit and do AI consulting full time?

## User Summary

- Recommendation type: `test`
- Recommendation: Do not quit immediately; run a bounded AI consulting validation sprint while protecting income and household stability.
- Core reason: The market may be real, but you do not yet have enough demand, offer, or runway evidence to justify a full-time jump.
- Biggest risk or objection: Burnout may make the job feel intolerable, but quitting without proof could transfer stress from work into money and marriage.
- First action: Within 48 hours, define one narrow AI consulting offer and discuss a stop-loss plan with your wife.

## The Real Decision

The surface question is whether to quit. The more important decision is whether AI consulting is already a credible replacement path, or whether it is still an attractive but unvalidated escape route.

Your desire to leave the current state is not weakness. It is real information. But that signal cannot, by itself, prove that quitting is the right move; it proves that the current setup needs a serious redesign.

## Options I See

- Stay and do nothing: safest in the short term, but likely to deepen resentment and burnout.
- Quit immediately: emotionally clean, but financially and relationally fragile unless demand is already proven.
- Validate AI consulting while employed: slower, but gives you evidence without forcing your household to absorb maximum risk.
- Redesign your job situation first: reduce work stress, start interviewing, or negotiate scope while validating consulting on the side.

## Key Judgment

I would not optimize the question as "quit or stay" yet, because it compresses the real decision too narrowly. The useful question is:

> Can you create enough external proof of AI consulting demand to justify replacing stable income?

Right now, the best answer is a capped test, not a full commitment.

## Strongest Objection

The strongest objection is that you may be too burned out to run a side validation sprint well. If your current job leaves no real energy, a part-time test may understate the opportunity and overstate your lack of discipline.

That objection changes the plan but does not justify immediate quitting. The first part of the test should validate capacity, not just market demand.

## Final Recommendation

Recommendation type: `test`

Run a 30-day capacity-and-demand test before making any resignation decision.

The bet is simple: if AI consulting is a real near-term path, you should be able to produce some concrete signal within 30 days: a clear offer, real buyer conversations, at least one serious lead, or a small paid pilot. If you cannot produce any signal, quitting would be based more on pain than evidence.

## Test Plan

### 48 Hours

- Define one narrow offer, such as "AI workflow audit for small service businesses" or "internal GPT automation for recruiting teams."
- Write down the minimum evidence required before quitting: runway target, paid pilot target, and spouse agreement.
- Have the stop-loss conversation with your wife before taking external action.

### 7 Days

- Contact 10 potential buyers or warm connectors with a simple offer.
- Book at least 3 discovery conversations.
- Track energy honestly: whether this gives you energy, drains you further, or only feels good as fantasy.

### 30 Days

- Try to close 1 paid pilot or 2 strong unpaid pilots with explicit buyer commitment.
- Decide whether the next step is continuing the test, changing the offer, job-searching, or reducing work stress first.
- Do not resign unless demand evidence and household stop-loss conditions are both met.

### Later / Optional

- Build a public case study only after at least one real buyer conversation.
- Explore a job switch if burnout remains high but consulting demand is weak.
- Create a longer runway plan if the first 30 days show real demand.

## Communication Script

"I want to talk about this in a way that does not dump risk on you. I am feeling strongly that my current work setup is not sustainable, but I agree that quitting without proof would put too much pressure on us. My goal is not to force a leap; it is to test whether AI consulting is real enough to deserve a bigger move. I want us to define the conditions together: how much runway we need, what proof of demand counts, and what would make us stop. For the next 30 days, I am proposing a bounded test, not a resignation decision. If the test does not produce evidence, I will not treat my frustration as proof that quitting is right. I want your concerns in the plan, not outside the plan."

## Stop Conditions

- Stop the test if sleep, health, or job performance materially worsens for two straight weeks.
- Do not quit if there is no paid pilot, serious lead, or repeatable buyer signal after 30 days.
- Do not quit if your wife does not agree that the stop-loss conditions are clear.
- Reconsider the plan if a strong job-change path appears faster and safer than consulting.

## What Would Make This Wrong

This advice would change if you already have committed buyers, a much larger runway than stated, a job situation that is actively damaging your health, or a legal/employment constraint that prevents any side validation.
